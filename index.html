    Why
    Why

    One individual

    Only one individual can acquire the work. For the artist, this is not a strategy but a necessary way of working. Only when he knows for whom he is creating does complete focus emerge. The deliberate choice brings clarity and dedication to the making process. Without an addressee, he does not begin. Within that concentration lies his full strength.

    The artist works in solitude and chooses one specific individual, time after time. For that person, one unique object comes into being — exclusive and non-transferable. The receiver is free to buy the work or to refuse it, and determines its value alone. Each choice forms an autonomous moment within an ongoing series.
    What
    What

    One work

    CustomerFirst is an ongoing art project built on one radical principle: each work is created for one specifically chosen individual. No open sale, no target audience, no market mechanism. The artist chooses. Only that person can acquire the work and determines the price entirely on their own — or decides not to. Every outcome is part of the work.

    The project reverses the conventional logic of value. There is maximum exclusivity, yet complete autonomy on the other side. No deadline, no negotiation, no pressure. The purchase or refusal is not incidental, but a layer of meaning within the work.

    How
    How

    One moment of value

    When the object approaches its final stage, it is time to initiate contact. A letter. In hard copy. Delivered through a carefully selected intermediary. This intermediary is informed of how the letter functions within the project — and is then entirely free to decide what to do with it. No obligation. No instruction. No pressure.

    Only when the letter reaches its destination and is read does the object assume its full meaning. Until that moment, it exists in suspension. The act of delivery — or the silence that follows — completes the work.

    Insights
    Insights

    A deliberately narrowed frame.

    Structurally eliminated

    This project barely fits a traditional business plan. Of the fifty standard components you would normally fill in — market analysis, scaling strategy, competitive positioning, distribution, exit scenario — thirty are simply not applicable. Not because they are missing, but because they are irrelevant. There is no target audience: there is a selection. No pricing strategy: the responsibility for the price lies with the receiver. No distribution question: there is one specific receiver. What remains is not a business model but a concentrated proposition, in which scarcity and intention outweigh scale.

    The power game, reversed

    In virtually every market, the maker tries to convince the buyer, the price is fixed in advance, and scarcity is manufactured. Here the opposite happens. The buyer is chosen. The price is not imposed but determined. The scarcity is absolute: one work, one person. The artist keeps full autonomy, while the buyer receives maximum recognition. What emerges is not a negotiation, but a controlled transfer of meaning — and precisely therein lies its power.

    “Value does not arise from scale.
    Value arises from concentration.”

    More why
    The maker

    It began with a simple question.

    What am I truly good at? Not what I'm capable of. Not what I've been trained to do. But what I can do effortlessly, with dedication, and without watching the clock. The answer was clear: I am at my best when I work with my hands. With materials that have already lived a life. Wood that carries marks. Metal with a past. Leftover pieces no one needed anymore — except me.

    I prefer to create something built so carefully that you can see how much attention it holds. Not a polished end product that hides its own making — but an object in which labor is visible. Tangible. Honest.

    In my professional world, I have heard the same words for years: customer centric, customer first, customer success. They are presented as the highest principle. Yet rarely is the relationship made more important than the model. That felt strange. What if customer first is not a strategy — but a starting point? What if you don't begin with a product and then search for a buyer, but begin with a person?

    Then everything aligned. I select one person first. Then I create an object that exists only for that person. No series. No inventory. No webshop. When the work is finished, there is only one possibility: that one person can buy it. Or not.

    This project is not a gallery. Not a product line. Not a traditional art practice. It is an exploration of attention. Of dedication. Of what happens when you give your full focus to one individual.

    Maybe one day, you are that person. Maybe not.
